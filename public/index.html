"Our ICP is defined." The head of sales at a portfolio company (Series A, $2.9m revenue) told me this recently with complete confidence, but what they'd actually defined was two job titles joined by an "or": VP of Engineering or Head of Platform. That's a persona, and starting with the persona is the right instinct, because they carry the job-to-be-done and feel the pain you are leaning into. But a persona on its own is not a target. The same title is a different buyer depending on the company behind it. For example, a Head of Engineering at Facebook and a Head of Engineering at a 40-person startup share almost nothing: different budget, authority, pain, reason to move. Same job, opposite drivers. The persona tells you who feels the pain, but the ICP tells you which companies make that pain funded, urgent and repeatable. Skip that second half, and you hit one of two walls: --> Happy ears: you get the meeting, the nod, the "this really matters", and then nothing moves. No driver behind the pain, no budget, no mandate. You read enthusiasm as pipeline, and it dies in "no decision." --> Or silence: you can't get the meeting at all, because your positioning speaks to a driver this persona doesn't carry here. Low replies, dead outbound, "not a priority." Both look exactly like a product problem, and many decide they haven't found product-market fit and go back to build more, or pivot away. Look, often the pain is real, the persona is right, and the account is wrong. That is not a PMF gap to me, but an ICP and positioning gap that looks identical from the inside. Same product, put in front of the company with the driver, moves. So before you rebuild the product, check that you are not selling the right pain to the wrong company.
